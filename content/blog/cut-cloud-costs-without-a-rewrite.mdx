Cloud bills rarely grow because of one big mistake. They grow through small decisions nobody revisits: a database sized for a launch that never came, a test environment left running, logs kept forever. The fix is rarely a rewrite. It is a steady, low-risk clean-up in the right order.

## Step 1: Make spend visible

You can't reduce what you can't see. Before changing anything:

- **Tag every resource** by service, environment and team.
- **Set a budget and alerts** per environment, so surprises show up in days, not at month-end.
- **Name an owner** for each line of the bill.

This step alone often reveals forgotten resources that can simply be switched off.

## Step 2: Remove what nobody uses

Look for the easy wins first. They carry almost no risk:

- Test and preview environments running at night and at weekends.
- Unattached disks, old snapshots and unused IP addresses.
- Logs and backups kept far longer than any rule requires.

## Step 3: Right-size what's left

Many servers and databases run well below their capacity. Check average and peak use over a few weeks, then step down one size at a time, watching performance as you go. For workloads that rise and fall, use auto-scaling instead of sizing for the peak all day.

## Step 4: Commit where it's predictable

Once usage is stable and right-sized, use your provider's commitment discounts (reserved capacity or savings plans) for the steady baseline. Keep the spiky part on demand. Commit after right-sizing, never before, or you lock in the waste.

## Step 5: Fix the expensive patterns in the code

Now look at how the application uses resources:

- **Chatty services** making many small network calls that could be one.
- **Missing caches** for data that rarely changes.
- **Heavy work done during user requests** that belongs in a background queue.
- **Data transfer** between regions or out to the internet that could be avoided.

These changes take more effort, but they improve speed as well as cost.

## Step 6: Keep it from creeping back

Cost control is a habit, not a project. Review the bill monthly, show cost per customer or per transaction next to your business metrics, and add cost checks to architecture reviews for new features.

## A note on green software

Most of these steps also cut energy use. Less idle capacity and fewer wasted cycles mean a smaller carbon footprint, which is increasingly something customers and regulators ask about.
