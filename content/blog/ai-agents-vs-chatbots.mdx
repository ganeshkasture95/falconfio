"Agent" is one of the most overused words in software right now. Plenty of products called agents are really chatbots with a new label. The difference matters, because it decides what the software can actually do for your team.

## A chatbot talks. An agent acts.

A **chatbot** takes a question and returns an answer. Good ones answer from your own documents and cite their sources. That is genuinely useful for support and internal knowledge, but the work still ends with a person reading the answer and doing something.

An **AI agent** takes a goal and completes it. It reads the situation, plans the steps, uses your tools (the CRM, the inbox, the calendar, the database) and checks its own work. A person comes in only when the agent is unsure or the stakes are high.

In short:

- **Input:** a chatbot gets a question; an agent gets a goal or an event.
- **Output:** a chatbot gives an answer; an agent gets work done in your systems.
- **Tools:** a chatbot usually has none; an agent uses your CRM, email, calendar, databases and APIs.
- **Your role:** with a chatbot you read every answer; with an agent you approve the important steps.

## A simple example

A lead fills in your website form.

- A **chatbot** could answer the lead's questions about pricing.
- An **agent** researches the company, scores the lead against your ideal customer, drafts a personal reply, checks your calendar, books a call and logs everything in the CRM. If the lead looks like a large deal, it asks a salesperson before replying.

## How agents stay safe

Agents are powerful because they can act, and that is also why they need guardrails. The ones we build always have:

- **Limited access.** Each agent can use only the tools and data its job needs.
- **Human approval for important steps.** You decide where a person signs off.
- **An audit log.** Every step, tool call and decision is recorded.
- **Tests before launch.** Evaluation suites check accuracy and safety on real examples.
- **Protection against manipulation.** Requests that try to trick the agent are blocked and logged.

## Picking your first agent

The best first agent is not the most impressive one. It's the one that pays back fastest. Look for work that is:

1. **Frequent.** It happens many times a day or week.
2. **Rule-based enough.** A new hire could do it with a good checklist.
3. **Spread across tools.** People copy information from one system to another.
4. **Low risk to get slightly wrong.** A mistake is caught and fixed easily.

Support triage, lead qualification, document processing and meeting follow-ups usually tick all four.
